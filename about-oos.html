Chapter One · The Early Years

Where It All Began

The story behind Olson Outdoor Services begins long before the company itself.

Its roots can be traced to the early 1970s, when Lyle Olson began establishing his career in grounds maintenance, working with property management companies throughout the Twin Cities metropolitan area.

Among his early professional experiences was serving as Grounds Maintenance Supervisor at the Colony of Richfield.

It was during these formative years that his appreciation for landscaping developed into something more than a profession. He recognized the value of understanding the land, the plants that grew upon it, and the craftsmanship required to maintain them properly.

In 1974, determined to deepen his knowledge and pursue his interest in horticulture, Lyle enrolled at Anoka Technical College.

He completed its two-year landscaping program, earning his diploma and building upon the practical experience he had already gained in the field.

Armed with that education, a strong work ethic, and an independent spirit, he set out to establish a business of his own.

Chapter Two · Building a Reputation

An Old Pickup Truck and a Few Shovels

Like many small businesses, Lyle Olson Landscapes began with modest resources and considerable determination.

With an old pickup truck, a few shovels, and a willingness to take on whatever work came his way, Lyle began building his business one property and one professional relationship at a time.

His early work encompassed everything from general landscaping and grounds maintenance to parking lot asphalt repairs.

As his experience grew, he increasingly focused on landscape design, plant care, and the specialized art of shrub trimming.

In 1989, he further expanded his horticultural knowledge by earning his Master Gardener certification through the University of Minnesota.

Over the years, Lyle became particularly respected for his meticulous approach to shrub trimming.

He understood that different varieties of shrubs required different approaches, and that properly shaping a plant meant respecting its natural characteristics rather than treating every shrub the same.

He believed that good landscaping required more than equipment and physical effort. It demanded knowledge, patience, and an eye for detail.

That philosophy became a defining characteristic of his work.

Property management companies and homeowners associations came to recognize his craftsmanship, and many sought his services repeatedly because of the pride and precision he brought to their properties.

For Lyle, his reputation was never separate from his workmanship. Every property reflected his personal commitment to the trade, and every completed job carried his name.

Pride in workmanship is the best boss you will ever have.

Chapter Three · Growing Up Olson

Three Sons and a Family Education

Although Lyle Olson Landscapes remained an independently owned, single-owner business, its influence extended well beyond the customers it served.

It was also where Lyle's three sons received their earliest education in the landscaping trade.

Their introduction to the business wasn't always glamorous. It often began with being dragged out of bed early in the morning, climbing into the work truck, and accompanying their father to properties throughout the metropolitan area.

For one of those boys, the bicycle his father tossed into the back of the truck was nearly as important as the landscaping equipment.

While his father and the crew worked, there were friends to be made around the properties and small adventures to be had.

Occasionally, he would be handed a five-dollar bill and sent riding his bicycle to a nearby gas station to bring back cold drinks for the crew.

Lyle even developed his own rather humorous compensation arrangement for his young helper.

He offered two dollars a day: one dollar for helping with the work and another dollar for staying out of his hair.

It was an arrangement that suited a young boy just fine.

After carefully saving fourteen dollars, that boy eventually had enough money to purchase a remote-controlled car from a thrift store during a stop on the way home.

What seemed like ordinary childhood moments at the time would eventually become treasured memories of growing up around a family business.

As the boys grew older, their involvement became more meaningful.

Lyle taught with a combination of patience and old-school firmness. He expected his sons to pay attention, learn the names of the shrubs they worked with, and understand why different plants required different care.

He was equally particular about the quality of their work.

Above all, he made certain they understood that his name was on every job they completed.

It was an education in both a profession and personal responsibility.

Chapter Four · The Family Foundation

The Lessons of Two Parents

While Lyle was teaching his sons the value of workmanship, their mother, Debra Jones, was providing another kind of foundation.

A mother and caretaker, Debra understood that love was measured not simply by what a person said, but by what they did for others.

Through some of life's most difficult moments, she was a source of comfort, encouragement, and emotional strength.

When the road ahead seemed uncertain, her love and support provided a reassuring light through the darkness.

Debra Jones

Her influence carried a lesson every bit as lasting as those learned on the landscaping properties.

Love isn't just a word. It's something we demonstrate through our actions.

Together, Lyle and Debra helped shape values that would take on deeper meaning as their sons grew older.

From their father came an appreciation for knowledge, hard work, accountability, and pride in craftsmanship.

From their mother came an understanding of compassion, loyalty, and the importance of standing beside the people who matter.

Different lessons, perhaps, but both rooted in the belief that character is revealed through actions.

Lyle eventually retired from his landscaping business around 2010, bringing to a close a career built through decades of dedication to his profession.

He passed away in 2019.

But the knowledge he shared, the standards he maintained, and the lessons both parents instilled would continue to shape the next chapter of the Olson story.

Chapter Five · A New Generation

A New Chapter — Olson Outdoor Services LLC

Growing up around my father's business gave me something that I didn't fully appreciate until later in life: a foundation of hands-on knowledge and experience that had been passed down to me over many years.

But it wasn't just the landscaping knowledge that stayed with me.

It was watching the relationships my father built with property managers and homeowners associations.

His customers came to know him personally. They trusted his judgment, respected his experience, and understood the care he brought to their properties.

As I grew older, I began recognizing the value of those relationships and the craftsmanship behind them.

I also began to realize that the kind of knowledge my father practiced—the ability to understand plants, recognize their individual needs, and take the time to perform quality work—was something I felt was becoming increasingly rare.

I knew I possessed a skill worth preserving and an opportunity to build something meaningful of my own.

That realization became part of the inspiration behind Olson Outdoor Services LLC.

My journey to this point hasn't always followed a straight road.

There have been difficult chapters, unexpected turns, and times when finding my direction seemed farther away than I wanted it to be.

But somewhere along that journey, I began to recognize that many of the values my parents had spent years trying to teach me had never really left.

They were still there.

My father's insistence on taking pride in my work. His belief that a person's name and reputation should mean something.

My mother's example of showing love and care through actions rather than words alone.

Those lessons took time to fully appreciate, but they became part of the foundation upon which I'm building my life and business today.

Olson Outdoor Services is not an inherited business or a continuation of Lyle Olson Landscapes.

It is a new, independently owned company, shaped by the experiences and values that came before it.

My goal is to build lasting professional relationships with the people who entrust their properties to my care.

I want the property managers, homeowners associations, and customers I work with to know me personally.

I want them to feel comfortable asking questions, value my judgment, and know that I take their concerns seriously.

To me, success isn't measured only by how many properties a company maintains.

It's also measured by the trust it earns, the relationships it develops, and the reputation it builds along the way.

Chapter Six · The Roots That Remain

The Roots That Remain

There are moments in life when the people who shaped us feel especially close, even when they aren't standing beside us.

For me, a monarch butterfly has become a personal reminder of my father.

When one appears, I often feel a sense of his presence and a connection to the memories and lessons he left behind.

A dragonfly holds a similar meaning for my mother, reminding me of her love, her strength, and the comfort she has given me throughout my life.

These are small but meaningful reminders of the people whose influence helped shape the person I've become.

I sometimes wonder what my father would think of the business I'm building today.

I hope that, despite the winding road it took me to get here, he would recognize something familiar in the way I approach my work.

Not perfection. Not a recreation of his business.

But the values he worked so hard to teach me, finally finding their place in my life.

My father started with an old pickup truck, a few shovels, and a belief that good workmanship could build a good name.

Decades later, I'm beginning a chapter of my own, carrying forward that same belief.

His name was on every job he completed. Today, that same family name is on mine.

And I intend to honor it.
